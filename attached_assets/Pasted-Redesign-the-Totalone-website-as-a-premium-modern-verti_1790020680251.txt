Redesign the Totalone website as a premium, modern vertical SaaS website for the furniture retail industry.

POSITIONING

Totalone is a management platform built specifically for furniture retailers, interior design showrooms and multi-store furniture businesses.

It connects the complete operational workflow:

Lead → Customer → Quote → Project → Orders → Payments → Logistics → Installation → After-sales → Performance

Do NOT position Totalone as a generic CRM or ERP.

The core promise is:

One shared way of working for the entire company.

Totalone helps furniture businesses move from fragmented information across Excel, WhatsApp, email, paper and individual knowledge to a shared operational system.

The website should make visitors think:

“These people understand exactly how a furniture business works.”

TARGET CUSTOMERS

The website must work for businesses at different stages:

* Small showroom: 3–4 people, owner keeps much of the information personally. Needs simplicity and fewer forgotten details.
* Established showroom: 5–10 people. Owner is constantly interrupted because information is fragmented.
* Growing company: 8–15 people. Needs shared processes and easier delegation.
* Multi-store business: needs visibility across people, projects and locations without the owner coordinating everything.
* Structured company: already has software but still manages many activities through Excel, email and parallel processes.
* Large retail group: needs standardized workflows, comparable information and operational visibility across locations.

Do not explicitly segment the homepage by revenue. Speak about increasing organizational complexity.

DESIGN

Create a premium international SaaS aesthetic.

Take inspiration from the design quality of Linear, Attio, Stripe and modern vertical SaaS products, without copying them.

Use:

* large typography
* generous whitespace
* strong grid
* subtle borders
* sophisticated micro-interactions
* excellent hierarchy
* restrained animations
* premium typography
* clean responsive design

Avoid traditional ERP aesthetics, generic corporate blue SaaS design, excessive gradients, glassmorphism, huge icon grids and startup clichés.

The product should feel simple but powerful, not technical or intimidating.
